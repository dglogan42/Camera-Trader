Stolen goods — NZ resources

      

        

          There is no public NZ Police API to look up camera serial numbers.
          Police publish public stolen lists for vehicles and boats only.
          Serials matter for reports and for licensed secondhand dealers’ records.
        

        

          Demo “Check stolen” uses a local mock list only.
          Suspected stolen gear must be handled via official / commercial channels below — not this app alone.
        


        
SNAP (snap.org.nz) — decommissioned

        

          SNAP (Serial Number Action Partnership) was a New Zealand Police initiative
          (site since ~2002) where people could store serial numbers of belongings in case of theft.
          Secondhand buyers/dealers could sometimes check serials against reported stolen property.
        

        

          The SNAP website was turned off on 15 December 2021.
          snap.org.nz is no longer an active Police service — do not build production
          checks against it. Police instead recommend keeping your own records (e.g. photos of
          serial plates and identifying marks) and reporting theft via 105.

Best practice (Ecotechnologies / NZ)

        

          Receiving stolen goods is an offence. Buy from people of good character or reputable organisations.
          Anyone dealing in secondhand goods for more than six days a year generally needs a secondhand dealer licence.
          Record model + serial, keep photos and receipts to prove ownership and aid recovery.

Secondhand dealers (stores)

        

          Licensed dealers under the Secondhand Dealers and Pawnbrokers Act 2004 must keep dealers’ records
          (seller ID, description, serial number, price, etc.) and report goods known or suspected stolen.
          Items acquired typically must be held before resale (see Act / Police business guidance).
